# Grill My Resume

Deliver a candid hiring-level assessment, not encouragement disguised as feedback. Be direct without being insulting. Expose weak positioning, unsupported claims, buried evidence, generic language, ATS problems, and reasons a recruiter might reject the CV.

## Establish the evidence

1. Read the complete CV. Preserve dates, titles, metrics, and exact facts.
2. If the source is PDF or DOCX and the corresponding document skill is available, use it to extract and visually inspect the artifact. Check both content and rendered layout.
3. Read any supplied job description, candidate dossier, portfolio, LinkedIn profile, or project repository. If the job is provided by URL or may have changed, verify the live posting before assessing fit.
4. Distinguish verified facts, reasonable inferences, and missing evidence. Never turn an inference into a CV claim.
5. If context is missing, complete the useful general review first. Ask only questions whose answers would materially change the verdict or rewrite.

## Review through two filters

### Ten-second recruiter scan

Determine whether the top third immediately communicates:

- target role and seniority;
- strongest relevant capabilities;
- credible proof of delivery or impact;
- location, language, or work-mode information when relevant;
- a coherent career story.

State what the recruiter is likely to assume, including unfavorable assumptions caused by ambiguity.

### Hiring-manager interrogation

Challenge every important line:

- What was built, changed, owned, or solved?
- What was the candidate's personal contribution?
- What scale, constraint, complexity, or consequence made it meaningful?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- Is the wording specific enough to discuss in an interview?
- Does the bullet show engineering or domain depth, or merely list duties and tools?

Flag inflated titles, vague ownership, buzzword piles, suspicious metrics, unexplained transitions, chronology gaps, and technologies named without demonstrated use.

## Assess role fit realistically

Do not treat a job description as a literal checklist or require a 100% match. Employers often describe an idealized candidate, and many capabilities are learned in-role.

Classify requirements as:

- **Hard blocker:** legally or operationally indispensable, such as mandatory authorization, location, license, language, clearance, or a genuinely essential specialist credential.
- **Core evidence:** capabilities the CV should prove directly or through strong transferable work.
- **Learnable gap:** missing experience that can plausibly be acquired after hiring.
- **Wishlist:** weakly predictive preferences or inflated posting language.

A candidate with roughly 20–40% direct overlap may still merit an application when the fundamentals, learning velocity, and transferable evidence are strong. Judge trajectory and adjacent capability, not only title similarity. Never steer a technically capable candidate toward administrative work merely because an older title or operational task makes that an easy match.

## Score the CV

Score each dimension from 0 to 5 and justify every score with evidence:

1. Positioning and target-role clarity
2. Relevance to the target
3. Evidence, ownership, and impact
4. Technical or domain credibility
5. Career narrative and progression
6. Readability and information hierarchy
7. ATS resilience and keyword coverage
8. Trustworthiness and interview defensibility

Give an overall verdict, but do not hide behind the number. State the three issues most likely to cost interviews.

## Inspect presentation and ATS behavior

Check page count, density, typography, spacing, alignment, hierarchy, consistency, link readability, contact details, date formats, and scan order. Check whether essential content depends on columns, tables, icons, headers, footers, graphics, or unusual characters that may parse badly.

Do not claim that one exact ATS format is universally required. Identify concrete parsing risks and propose a simpler alternative when warranted.

## Rewrite under factual constraints

When proposing copy:

- preserve factual meaning and do not invent metrics, employers, customers, scope, seniority, or technologies;
- lead bullets with outcomes, ownership, or hard problems rather than passive duties;
- connect tools to what they enabled;
- prefer precise plain language over adjectives and buzzwords;
- surface independent products, open-source work, and credible technical projects as experience when they demonstrate real delivery;
- label placeholders such as `[add verified user count]` instead of fabricating proof;
- optimize for the target role while keeping every line defensible in an interview.

If asked to edit the actual file, use the relevant PDF or document skill, preserve an untouched source copy, render the result, and visually verify it before delivery.

## Required output

Present the review in this order:

1. **Verdict:** interview likelihood and the clearest reason.
2. **Ten-second read:** what a recruiter sees and assumes.
3. **Top three rejection risks:** specific and prioritized.
4. **Scorecard:** eight scores with concise evidence.
5. **Role-fit map:** hard blockers, core evidence, learnable gaps, and wishlist items when a target role exists.
6. **Line-by-line grill:** quote or identify the weak line, explain the problem, then give a factual replacement.
7. **What is missing:** evidence or questions that would materially strengthen the CV.
8. **Fix order:** changes grouped into `before applying`, `high leverage`, and `polish`.

End with a clear recommendation: apply now, apply after specific fixes, or do not apply because of a named hard blocker. Never recommend against applying solely because the CV does not match every listed requirement.
